**Garlic confit.** Two heads, peeled, cloves whole. Enough neutral oil to cover them by a finger's
width. One bay leaf if you have it.

Lowest heat the hob will hold. **The oil must never sizzle.** An occasional lazy bubble is correct; a
steady fizz means it is frying, and fried garlic is a different and much less interesting ingredient.
45 minutes, until a clove collapses under a spoon with no resistance.

Cool completely in the oil. Fridge, submerged, two weeks. The oil is the better half of the recipe -
use it on bread, in dressing, over roast potatoes, anywhere you would otherwise add garlic and heat at
the same time.

**Why it works:** nothing browns below about 140C, so at a low enough temperature you get an hour of
cooking with none of the reactions that make garlic sharp and bitter. The sugars caramelise, the harsh
sulphur compounds cook off, and what is left is sweet and spreadable.

Keep it cold and eat it inside two weeks. Garlic in oil at room temperature is a genuine botulism
risk, and this is the one instruction here with no room for interpretation.
