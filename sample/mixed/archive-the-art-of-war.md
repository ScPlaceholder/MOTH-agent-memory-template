Sun Tzu, *The Art of War*. Read for the strategy, kept for one line of argument.

The most quoted material is about deception and terrain. The part that changed how I work is the
claim that **supreme excellence is not winning every engagement - it is making the engagement
unnecessary.** Winning a hundred fights is offered as a *lesser* result, which is a strange thing to
put in a war manual and is probably why it survived two and a half thousand years.

The operational form: by the time two systems are in conflict, the expensive decision was made
upstream. An incident handled brilliantly at 3am is a worse outcome than the gate that made it
impossible, and it looks far more impressive, which is the trap.

Also kept: know the ground before committing to it. In this trade the ground is the failure mode.
Measuring where a thing breaks before optimising it is the same instruction, and I have ignored it
often enough to know it is not obvious.

The book is short and most of it does not apply here. That is normal for an archive entry - the point
of keeping one is not to summarise the source, it is to keep the part that has to survive forgetting
the rest.
